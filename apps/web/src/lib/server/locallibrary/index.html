locallibrary

locallibrary looks after your rekordbox library and the USB drives that carry it. If you export from rekordbox to a stick and play from it on CDJs or XDJs, it can:

  • keep every copy of your library identical to the drive you export to, copying only what changed;
  • tell you which drive is newest, and why;
  • keep a history of your library on your computer, so a lost or broken drive can be rebuilt;
  • edit playlists, tags, cues, ratings and cover art right on your drives, and redo those edits after rekordbox exports again;
  • check drives before a set, down to the tracks a particular player won't load;
  • keep the history lists players record and turn them into tracklists for Mixcloud, YouTube or a CUE sheet;
  • set the same My Settings on every drive, and format drives the way players read them.

It runs in a terminal, on a Mac (Linux and Windows are experimental). Typing locallibrary on its own opens a player where you can browse your library and listen to it the way a CDJ shows it. Every change shows its plan and asks first, and locallibrary undo takes the last one back.

Getting it

There's no ready-made download yet, so for now you build it on your own computer. Installing locallibrary walks through it. Then plug in your drives and run: 

locallibrary setup

The README covers everyday use, and the guides cover the rest.

Signing in

This address is how locallibrary introduces itself when you sign in with your Atmosphere account, the account you use for Bluesky and other AT Protocol apps. You sign in on your own account's server, in your browser: locallibrary never sees your password.

Signing in only asks to know who you are. Sign out with locallibrary account logout, or remove locallibrary from your account's settings, at any time.

Publishing

Publishing your sets, your My Settings or your DJ profile asks for one more permission, the first time: to create and update those three kinds of record in your account. It can't delete them, and can't touch anything else.

  • locallibrary sets publish writes a set: the day, venue, event and tracks in order.
  • locallibrary settings publish writes your My Settings.
  • locallibrary profile publish writes a rounded summary of how you play.

Each one shows exactly what it will publish and asks first. Tracks you mark unnamed go out without a name, and a set's notes, recordings and file paths stay on your computer. What's published is public.

A recorded mix exported with locallibrary mix … --export can go on your Local Network profile, with its tracklist, tempo and every transition.

The details servers read are in oauth-client-metadata.json.
